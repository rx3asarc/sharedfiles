# BRIEF 1 — Advanced nuclear / microreactor developers compared

## Context

I am evaluating publicly traded advanced-nuclear companies for a small, concentrated
portfolio. I need a **comparative** report, not profiles. My decision criteria are specific
and unusual, so please weight the report toward them.

## The companies

Obtain comparable data on **at minimum** these, plus any material ones I have missed:

- NuScale Power (SMR)
- Oklo (OKLO)
- X-Energy (XE)
- Nano Nuclear Energy (NNE)
- Terrestrial Energy (IMSR)
- Westinghouse — **not directly investable**, see question 5
- Any other publicly listed microreactor or SMR developer

## The questions that decide it

**1. REGULATORY STATUS — the most important question.**

For each company, state precisely:
- Does it hold **US NRC design certification** or **standard design approval**? If yes, for
  which specific design and at what power rating, and on what date?
- If not, what stage is it at — pre-application, docketed, under review, submitted?
- Does it hold any non-US regulatory approval (UK ONR, Canada CNSC, etc.)?

I want to know who has actually cleared a regulator versus who has announced intentions.

**2. FUEL DEPENDENCY — the second most important question.**

For each: does the design require **HALEU** (high-assay low-enriched uranium) or
**conventional LEU**?
- If HALEU: who supplies it, under what agreement, with what delivery date, and is that
  supplier currently producing at scale?
- Note that HALEU supply in the West is extremely concentrated. I want to know which
  companies are exposed to that single point of failure and which are not.

**3. CAPITAL STRUCTURE AND DILUTION HISTORY.** For each company:
- Cash and marketable securities, most recent quarter
- Quarterly cash burn from operations and capex
- Revenue, most recent quarter, and trailing twelve months
- **Every equity raise in the last 24 months**: date, size, instrument, price relative to
  the prevailing share price. Include at-the-market (ATM) programs and their size and
  remaining capacity.
- **Shares outstanding today versus 24 months ago** — the actual dilution experienced.
- Any going-concern disclosure ever made.

I care about this more than the technology. A company that must continuously sell shares is
a different investment from one that does not.

**4. OFFTAKE — BINDING VERSUS NON-BINDING.**

For each announced agreement, classify it precisely:
- **Binding** contract / purchase order / definitive agreement
- **Non-binding**: MOU, LOI, master agreement, framework, letter of intent
- Any prepayment or cash received — amount and date

Give total binding versus total non-binding capacity in GW or MW. Do not sum them together.
For each non-binding agreement, state whether the counterparty has any obligation.

**5. WESTINGHOUSE / eVinci SPECIFICALLY.**

- Current ownership structure between Cameco and Brookfield Renewable
- Status of the proposed IPO: the draft S-1 submission, any stated timeline, and what the
  US government's $80bn partnership entails, including any warrants and the conditions
  attached (I have seen reference to a January 2029 final investment decision trigger —
  please verify)
- **The eVinci microreactor**: its power rating, its regulatory status, its fuel
  requirements, and its commercial timeline. Is there any way for a public-market investor
  to gain eVinci exposure other than through Cameco?

**6. TIMELINES — with dates and confidence.**

For each company, the earliest date it expects:
- first criticality of a commercial (not test) reactor
- first revenue from selling power
- and whether that date has slipped. If it has, give the original date and the new one.

**7. WHAT HAS GONE WRONG.**

For each company, list the material setbacks of the last 24 months: lost contracts, cancelled
awards, failed tests, regulatory rejections, executive departures, restatements. I want the
failure record, not the pitch.

## Output format

A comparison table across all companies covering: NRC status · fuel type · cash · quarterly
burn · revenue · total dilution over 24 months · binding GW · non-binding GW · earliest
commercial power date.

Then one short section per company on what would have to be true for it to work, and what
would falsify it.

**Every figure needs a source and a date.** Where sources disagree, show the disagreement.

# BRIEF 2 — Uranium exposure: vehicles compared

## Context

I want uranium exposure in a concentrated portfolio, but I want to understand the different
ways to get it and what each actually exposes me to. I am wary of companies that must
continuously raise equity.

## The vehicles

- **Cameco (CCJ)** — producer
- **Uranium Energy Corp (UEC)** — producer
- **Energy Fuels (UUUU)** — producer, also rare earths
- **Denison Mines (DNN)** — developer
- **Ur-Energy (URG)** and **enCore Energy (EU)** — smaller US producers
- **Uranium Royalty Corp (UROY)** — royalty model
- **Centrus Energy (LEU)** — enrichment
- **Sprott Physical Uranium Trust (U.UN / SRUUF)** — physical holding
- **Global X Uranium UCITS ETF (URNU, ISIN IE000NDWFGA5)** — index
- **Sprott Uranium Miners ETF (URNM)** — index
- Any other material listed vehicle

## Questions

**1. THE STRUCTURAL DIFFERENCE.** For each, explain what I actually own:
- A royalty over production?
- An operating mine with costs?
- A development project needing capex?
- Physical metal in a vault?
- An enrichment service business with government contracts?

**2. FUNDING AND DILUTION.** Same as Brief 1 question 3: cash, burn, revenue, all equity
raises in 24 months, share count change over 24 months, ATM capacity remaining.
**For the royalty and physical-holding vehicles specifically: is the model self-funding, or
does it require continuous capital raising?**

**3. PRODUCTION REALITY.** For each producer: current production in lbs U3O8, all-in
sustaining cost, contracted versus spot sales split, and average realized price. For
developers: capex required, permitting status, and financing status.

**4. SPOT VERSUS TERM MARKET.** Current spot price and current term price with dates and
sources. How much of each company's near-term production is already contracted, and at what
price? I want to understand which companies are actually exposed to the spot price versus
which have locked in revenue.

**5. THE DISCREPANCY.** Uranium spot rose roughly 6% between June and September 2026, while
uranium equities fell substantially. Please research **why**. Specifically: are producer
margins being eroded by cost inflation? Is there evidence the term price is set by a small
number of buyers? Are equity holders pricing in dilution, permitting delays, or something
else? Give sourced analysis, not speculation.

**6. THE UCITS ETF — URNU SPECIFICALLY.**
- Full current holdings with weights and as-of date
- Is it accumulating or distributing?
- Its TER
- **Does it have Austrian tax reporting status (Meldefonds)?** If this cannot be established,
  say so clearly — do not guess.

## Output

Comparison table: vehicle type · self-funding? · dilution over 24 months · production or
assets · contracted vs spot exposure · and the single main risk.

# BRIEF 3 — Austrian retail investor mechanics

## Context

I am an Austrian tax resident building a first, small equity portfolio. I need the practical
and tax mechanics resolved before I buy anything. **I understand this is not tax advice and I will confirm with a Steuerberater —
but I need to know the right questions and the current rules.**

## Questions

**1. PRIIPs.** Confirm precisely which products an Austrian retail investor can and cannot
buy. Specifically: are US-domiciled ETFs (URA, URNM) actually unavailable, and what
determines this? Are there exemptions or professional-investor routes?

**2. MELDEFONDS / REPORTING FUND STATUS.** Explain the distinction between reporting
(*Meldefonds*) and non-reporting funds under current Austrian law, and the actual tax
consequences of each. Then answer: **how does an investor verify a specific fund's
reporting status before buying?** Is there a public register, and where?

**3. AUSSCHÜTTUNGSGLEICHE ERTRÄGE.** Explain how accumulating funds are taxed annually in
Austria. What rate applies, on what base, and when is it due? What happens if the investor
has no cash distribution to pay it from?

**4. KESt.** Current rate. Is it withheld at source by Austrian brokers? What happens with a
foreign broker that does not withhold it — what must the investor then do?

**5. BROKERS FOR AUSTRIAN RESIDENTS.** Compare the main options on whether they handle KESt
withholding and Austrian tax reporting automatically. Include both Austrian banks and
international brokers available to Austrians. I specifically want to know which ones leave
the investor to file tax themselves.

**6. CURRENCY.** What are typical EUR→USD conversion costs at Austrian brokers, and does it
matter more for a small portfolio? Are there EUR-hedged or EUR-denominated alternatives for
global and uranium equity exposure?

**7. ANYTHING ELSE.** Any other Austrian-specific rule that materially affects a first-time
investor buying ETFs and individual foreign stocks — including loss offsetting rules,
reporting obligations, or deposit/account fees that matter at this portfolio size.

## Output

A practical checklist: what to verify before buying, what to ask a broker, what to ask a
Steuerberater. With sources and dates, and the standard caveat that rules change.

# HOW TO ASK FOR THIS

To each prompt, append:

> Please cite a source and a date for every figure. Where sources disagree, show both and
> say which you consider more reliable and why. If you cannot find a figure, write
> "not found" rather than estimating. Distinguish clearly between binding agreements and
> announcements of intent. Do not include analyst price targets or recommendations — I want
> facts and dates, not opinions.

**And when the reports come back:** send them to me. I will check the numbers against the
sources, flag anything that conflicts with the verified data already in this project, and
integrate what holds up.
