## The two questions that decide everything

**1 — Regulatory status.** Which companies actually hold NRC design certification versus
which have announced intentions?

> NuScale holds the **only** NRC-certified SMR design in the US. That is a permission-based
> moat: it cannot be bought or rushed — only submitted for, waited on, and passed. It is the
> one thing in this sector that a competitor cannot purchase.

**2 — Fuel dependency.** Which designs require **HALEU**, and who supplies it?

> HALEU supply in the West is extremely concentrated, and Centrus deliveries are targeted for
> 2029. Oklo's commercial timeline depends on that single point of failure. NuScale runs on
> conventional LEU and does not have that exposure.

Those two answers separate the sector more cleanly than any technology comparison.

## The tension the research should resolve

```
CCJ    self-funding  +  uranium  +  microreactor (49% of Westinghouse / eVinci)
LEU    self-funding  +  uranium enrichment (only Western HALEU producer at scale)
UROY   self-funding  +  uranium royalty (no capex, no dilution)

SMR    best moat (only NRC certification)  BUT  $10.7m revenue vs $418m expenses
OKLO   no NRC approval, perpetual ATM, timeline hostage to HALEU 2029
```

**Uranium exposure is easy to obtain with self-funding companies. Microreactor exposure is
not** — every pure-play is a funding-dependent story name.

The open question: **is there a self-funding route to microreactor exposure** — through
component suppliers, service providers, or a diversified owner? That is Brief 1, question 6.

## Where deep research tends to be weakest

Check these specifically when the reports come back:

- **Dilution math** — share count change over 24 months, ATM capacity used. It often reports
  announced programs rather than actual issuance.
- **Binding versus non-binding** — it tends to sum MOUs and LOIs with real contracts. Oklo's
  ~14 GW pipeline includes a 12 GW Switch agreement that is non-binding; it must not be
  added to firm commitments.
- **NRC status** — it often repeats company language rather than the regulatory docket. Ask
  for the specific design, power rating, and date.
- **Stale prices** — a stale figure looks authoritative. One document in this project carried
  a price table wrong for at least five tickers.
