## CONTEXT (read before answering)

I am comparing the different ways a public-market investor can get uranium exposure. I am
**wary of companies that must continuously raise equity to survive**, and I want to understand
what each vehicle actually exposes me to. I am not asking for a recommendation or a price
target — I want **facts, dates, and sourced comparisons**.

### The verified market facts you should start from (do not re-derive these)

As of mid-September 2026:

- **Uranium spot:** ~$85/lb in mid-June 2026 → **$89.99/lb on 11 September 2026** (+5.9%)
- **Term price:** ~$90–93/lb, described as a multi-decade high
- **The anomaly this brief exists to explain:** between 17 June and mid-September 2026, a
  24-name policy-catalyst basket containing many uranium names fell a **median of −17.5%**
  while the commodity rose. **The commodity rose and the equities fell.**

### The measured internal dispersion (use this to test your explanation)

```
uranium producers / royalty / enrichment   (n=8)    median   -5.6%
pre-revenue advanced-nuclear group         (n=8)    median  -27.3%
names that tapped equity markets           (n=4)    median  -33.1%
everything else                            (n=20)   median  -15.3%
```

**A 21.7-point gap existed *inside* the uranium complex** — so sub-sector alone does not
explain the result. A separate grouping suggested **self-funding** (profitable, no equity
raises) was the better predictor than commodity exposure:

```
profitable      (n=7)   median   -2.6%
unprofitable   (n=16)   median  -27.2%
```

**Known counterexamples that must be accounted for, not ignored:**

- a pre-revenue name **rose +4.1%**
- a company whose balance sheet was *repaired* mid-period (cash $16.6m → $91.1m) **still fell −53.7%**
- a genuine producing miner **fell −28.0%**, behaving like a story name

So the balance-sheet hypothesis is **suggestive, not proven.** Treat it as a hypothesis under
test, not a conclusion.

### 1. WHAT DO I ACTUALLY OWN?

For each vehicle, state plainly what the security represents:

- a **royalty** over someone else's production?
- an **operating mine** with real costs?

Then, for each: **is it self-funding from operations, or does it depend on capital markets?**
Answer with evidence, not impression.

### 2. FUNDING AND DILUTION — THE CENTRAL QUESTION

For **every** company on the list:

- Cash and marketable securities, most recent quarter, with the date
- Quarterly operating cash burn and capex
- Revenue: most recent quarter **and** trailing twelve months
- **Every equity raise in the last 24 months**: date, size, instrument, price relative to the
  prevailing share price. Include ATM programmes, their total size, how much was used, and
  how much remains.
- **Share count today versus 24 months ago** — the actual dilution experienced
- Ever had a going-concern disclosure? Is one active now?
- **Is free cash flow positive?** If not, how many quarters of runway remain at the current burn?

**For the royalty and physical-holding vehicles specifically:** is the model structurally
self-funding, or does it require continuous capital raising? This is the question I care most
about. Explain the mechanism, don't just assert it.

### 4. SPOT VERSUS TERM MARKET — WHERE REVENUE IS ACTUALLY SET

- Current **spot** price and current **term** price, each with date and source
- **How much of each producer's near-term output is already contracted, and at what price?**

- Which companies are genuinely exposed to spot, and which have locked in revenue?

I want to understand whether the equity market is pricing a *contracting* reality that the

### 5. THE DISCREPANCY — THE MOST IMPORTANT QUESTION

**Uranium spot rose ~6% between June and September 2026 while uranium equities fell
substantially. Why?**

Investigate and give sourced analysis, not speculation. Test these hypotheses explicitly and
say which the evidence supports:

1. **Cost inflation** — are producer margins being eroded by rising costs, so a higher uranium
   price does not translate into higher earnings?
2. **Term-market structure** — is the term price set by a few large buyers at prices that
   leave producers with thin margins regardless of spot?
3. **Dilution** — are equity holders pricing in future share issuance, so per-share value does
   not track the commodity?
4. **Permitting and execution** — are delays and regulatory constraints the binding factor
   rather than price?
5. **Liquidity and flow** — is this a positioning or fund-flow effect rather than a
   fundamental one?
6. **Something else** — say what, and evidence it.

**Also test this specific claim:** that **self-funding** companies outperformed
capital-dependent ones, regardless of commodity exposure. Use the measured dispersion above.
Give me the actual returns over 17 June → mid-September 2026 for each vehicle **if you can
source them**, and say where you cannot.
